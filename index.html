Four beats, one rally

Every ball you receive has four moments, each triggered by an event in the ball's flight rather than by a count. Hit each beat on its trigger and the app tells you, in milliseconds, which one you are chronically late on — and what that costs you at contact.

Space · “split”

Split step

Trigger: before their strings meet the ball

Press when you leave the ground — about 80 ms early, so you are in the air as they hit and land just after. Pure anticipation: you cannot react to this, only read the takeback.

← → · “turn”

Turn

Trigger: the instant you read the side

Your torso starts rotating — this is where the coil begins. Roughly 5 m of ball flight, long before the net. Wrong arrow is a miss.

↓ · “load”

Load

Trigger: the ball bounces

Everything arrives at once: coil finished, racket at the top of its loop, weight on the outside leg. Preparation ends here.

↑ · “hit”

Contact

Trigger: the ball is on the rise

Between the bounce and the top of the bounce. This is where a late turn finally shows up as a late, jammed ball — set exactly where you want it under Contact.

What each beat actually is — and what it isn't

The split is a jump

The press is when you leave the ground, roughly 80 ms before their strings meet the ball — not when you land. That is deliberate: the jump is the decision, the landing is its consequence. You cannot react to a shot that has not happened yet, so this beat is pure anticipation off their takeback, and the app varies their rhythm shot to shot so you cannot count it out.

Preparation is one movement

It is called a unit turn because the body and the racket turn as one — you get the racket back by turning your whole body, not by swinging the arm back. So there is no separate "now take the racket back" to time, and no beat for it here. The turn starts the whole thing; racket, shoulders and legs travel together from there.

Load is the finish line

At the bounce everything has arrived at once: rotation complete, racket at the top of its loop, weight on the outside leg, stance set. You are a coiled spring, briefly still. The bounce is what releases it.

Contact is a window

Between the bounce and the top of the bounce, the ball passes through your strike zone. Anywhere in there is a playable ball; where in there is a tactical choice, which is why it is a setting rather than a fixed target. Taking it early steals time from your opponent and punishes a late turn hardest.

The racket-head drop is still not a beat

Lowering the racket below the ball is the lag. It happens between Load and Hit, as you uncoil — passive, earned by being loaded on time and staying loose, not timed. That is why there is no cue for it.
